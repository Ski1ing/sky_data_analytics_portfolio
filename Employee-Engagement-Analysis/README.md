# Employee Engagement Analysis

## Project Overview
This project analyzes employee engagement survey data using Microsoft Excel to identify employee satisfaction, engagement, and departmental performance.
This project includes data cleaning, validation, PivotTables, interactive dashboard design, and business insights to help stakeholders understand overall
employee sentiment.

[Maven Analytics - Employee Survey Responses](https://app.mavenanalytics.io/datasets?search=Employee+)

## Tools Used
- Microsoft Excel
- PivotTables
- PivotCharts
- Slicers
- Excel Formulas

## Data Cleaning
- Checking for missing values
- Reviewing for duplicate records
- Standardized survey question text
- Identifying and correcting inconsistent responses
- Creating helper columns for data validation
- Excluding "Not Applicable" from average response calculations
- Verifying data consistency before analysis

## Business Insights
- The highest-rated survey question was: "I know what is expected of me at work." (Average: 3.49). This suggests employees generally understand their responsibilities and performance expectations.
- The lowest-rated survey question was: "I have a best friend at work." (Average: 2.60). While this question measures workplace connection rather than job performance, the low score indicates limited social engagement weaker interpersonal relationships among employees. Management may consider initiatives that encourage collaboration, team-building activities, and cross-department interaction to strengthen workplace culture.
- Despite variations between departments, the majority achieved response scores above 3.0, indicating generally favorable perceptions across much of the organization. This suggests that engagement challenges are concentrated in specific departments rather than an reflecting on organization-wide issues.
- Question related to employee recognition and opportunities to learn and grow received lower average scores than questions about role clarity. This may indicate that employee understand their responsibilities but feel that they receive less recognition or have fewer professional development. Strengthening recognition programs, expanding learning opportunities, and providing clearer career development pathways could help improve employee engagement.
